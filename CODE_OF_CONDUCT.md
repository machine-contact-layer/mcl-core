# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

MCL is intended to be implemented by people who work for competing
organisations, in different countries, with different priorities, over a long
period. That only works if disagreement stays technical.

## Expected behaviour

- Argue about the work, not the person proposing it.
- Assume the other participant has context you do not.
- Be specific. "This breaks on a Cortex-M0 because it assumes an allocator" is
  useful; "this is bad design" is not.
- Say what you actually verified and what you assumed. Overstated confidence
  costs more here than admitted uncertainty.
- Accept a technical decision that went against you, and raise it again only
  with new information.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Personal attacks, harassment, or demeaning comments about a person or group.
- Sustained disruption of technical discussion.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Pursuing an organisation's commercial interest while presenting it as a
  neutral technical argument.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, commit messages,
specification discussion — and whenever someone is representing the project
elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report a concern to the project maintainers through a private channel on the
hosting platform. Reports are handled confidentially.

This is a small private research project today. When MCL moves to multi-party
governance, this document will be replaced by the enforcement process described
in [`governance/ORGANIZATION_MODEL.md`](governance/ORGANIZATION_MODEL.md),
including a named contact and stated response times. Describing an enforcement
apparatus that does not yet exist would be its own form of dishonesty.
